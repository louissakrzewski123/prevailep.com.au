The basics

What is an Accredited Exercise Physiologist?

An AEP is a university-qualified allied health professional who uses movement and exercise to prevent and manage injury, disability and chronic conditions. We're recognised by Medicare, the NDIS, DVA and private health funds.

Think of an AEP as the bridge between the clinic and everyday life — translating your goals into safe, achievable movement that fits your body, your condition and your world. Not a gym program. A plan built for you.

What it actually looks like

Your first four weeks

Most people have never worked with an exercise physiologist and have no idea what to expect. Here's the honest version — no gym, no lycra, no being shouted at.

  1. Week 190 minutes

    We talk before we move

    I come to you. We sit down and go through your history, your diagnosis, what's changed, what you're finding hard and — most importantly — what you actually want to be able to do. Then some gentle baseline measures: how you stand up from a chair, how far you can walk comfortably, balance, grip. Nothing you can fail. You'll finish with two or three simple things to try.

  2. Week 260 minutes

    Your program, in your space

    I bring a plan built around your kitchen bench, your hallway, your back step — the things you already have. We do it together, adjust anything that doesn't feel right, and I write it up in plain language with photos so you're never guessing between sessions.

  3. Week 360 minutes

    Adjust, then progress

    Almost nothing survives contact with real life unchanged. We find what's fitting into your week and what isn't, and we fix it. This is usually the session where something clicks — a movement that felt impossible in week one starts feeling ordinary.

  4. Week 460 minutes

    Measure, and show your team

    We repeat the week-one baselines so you can see the change in numbers rather than guesswork. If you're an NDIS participant, you get a written progress report you can take to your plan review — clear evidence of what the funding is achieving.

After that, we settle into whatever rhythm suits you — weekly, fortnightly, or a block of sessions followed by a check-in. Your call, always.

Good to know

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a referral?

Not for NDIS or private sessions — you can reach out directly. For a Medicare CDM plan you'll need a referral from your GP, and DVA requires a referral (D904).

What areas do you cover?

In-home and community sessions across Brisbane and South-East Queensland, plus telehealth for clients anywhere in Australia.

Do I need to be fit or sporty?

Not at all. Programs start exactly where you are — many clients are completely new to structured exercise, and that's welcome.

What conditions do you work with?

A wide range, including neurological and developmental conditions, brain injury, chronic pain and disease, and mental health. If you're unsure whether I can help, just ask.

How much does it cost?

NDIS sessions are charged within the current NDIS price limits. Private, Medicare and DVA fees are provided on enquiry — I'll always be upfront about costs before we begin, never after.

How soon can I start?

I'm taking enquiries now. As a solo practice I keep my caseload deliberately small, so there's sometimes a short wait for a regular spot — but the free intro chat and initial assessment usually happen well before that. Get in touch and I'll tell you honestly where things stand rather than leaving you guessing.

My NDIS plan is NDIA-managed — can I still work with you?

Not at the moment. NDIA-managed (agency-managed) funding can only be spent with NDIS-registered providers, and Prevail isn't registered. You do have options though: you can request a change to plan management at your next review or via a plan variation — the NDIA funds plan management separately, so it doesn't come out of your other supports — or you can engage me privately. Ask me and I'll help you word the request.

Can you work with my support coordinator?

Absolutely, and I'd prefer to. I send progress reports ahead of plan reviews, keep coordinators in the loop on attendance and goals, and respond to referrals within one business day. There's a page for referrers with the details.

What if I have a bad day or need to cancel?

Life with a disability or chronic condition isn't predictable, and I don't expect it to be. Tell me as early as you can and we'll reschedule. If you're having a low-energy day but still want to go ahead, we do a lighter session — showing up on the hard days matters more than the program.
